Think first, then ask

New studies suggest that when you bring in AI matters as much as whether you do.

By Monty Montgomery · September 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Here's a question that sounds small: when you use AI for something you're writing, do you open it first, or after you've had a go yourself?

A growing pile of research suggests the order matters, for what you remember, for whether the work feels like yours, and even for whether you want to do it at all.

Ownership: is it still yours?

In the MIT Media Lab's essay-writing study, people who wrote with ChatGPT reported the lowest sense of ownership over their essays, and many couldn't accurately quote what they'd written. The study is small, not yet peer-reviewed, and its methods have been questioned, but its most interesting finding was about timing. In a final session with just 18 of the participants, people who had written on their own first and then got AI showed stronger brain engagement and better recall than people who went the other way.

A larger, pre-registered experiment published in 2026 makes the timing point even more clearly. People who simply copied AI-generated work reported lower confidence in their own abilities, less ownership, and less sense that the work mattered, and some of those effects lingered after they went back to working alone. But people who wrote their own draft first and then used AI to improve it felt about the same as people who never used AI at all.

Motivation: the boredom afterward

Across four studies with more than 3,500 people, researchers found that working with ChatGPT improved the quality of the work in front of people, but when they then had to do a task on their own, their intrinsic motivation dropped and boredom rose. The boost didn't carry over to the solo work. The flatness did.

Sameness: everyone starts to sound alike

There's a quieter cost too. In one study, stories written with AI ideas were rated more creative individually, but were more similar to each other. Another, with writers in India and the United States, found that AI writing suggestions nudged the Indian writers toward Western styles and flattened cultural detail. When everyone starts from the same machine, the starting points converge.

The rule of thumb

Put together, these studies point to a simple habit: think first, then ask. Have your own idea, write your own rough version, and then bring in the tool to sharpen it. You keep your voice, your memory of the work, and your reason to care about it, and you still get the help.
